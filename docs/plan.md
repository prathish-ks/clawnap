## Thesis and stop conditions (unchanged unless a gate fails)
Stock OpenClaw cells, unchanged, supervised by a small Go host-side control plane that hibernates idle cells and wakes them on inbound message, so a hosting provider runs 3–5x more cells per server.
Stop if: upstream ships hibernation or multi-host fleet; a credible open-source hibernation supervisor for OpenClaw on plain Docker hosts appears; two or more named providers exit; OpenClaw is abandoned or superseded; fewer than 2 of 10 providers name density a top-3 cost or will pay $500/mo; the Hetzner experiment cannot reach 3x baseline with an honest wake SLA.

## Decisions log
- 2026-10-02: operating envelope on the reference host (8 vCPU / 16 GB, 100 cells): at most ~15 % of cells resident at any instant: 5 % warm guaranteed (300 MiB, ~1 s wake), up to 10 % warm when memory allows, plus up to 10 cells mid-wake (700–800 MiB each for the 3-minute post-thaw window). Cold burst of ten 3–8 s. Optimisation of this host class stops here; further gains come from the provider's hardware or from an upstream post-thaw signal.
- 2026-10-01 (evening): operating rule: a woken cell stays awake at least 3 min and is not idle while above 10 % of a core (post-thaw maintenance). Recovery concurrency bounded at 4. Pressure reclaim yields to wakes in flight. Sizing rule unchanged; resident count self-adjusts to the host's available memory.
- 2026-10-01: design 1 adopted. Tiers: warm = paused cell reclaimed to the 300 MiB floor (hot set recorded), cold = 150 MiB floor with the hot set prefetched at wake. No memory.high cap. Sizing rule: RAM ≈ 1 GB + 50 MiB × cold + 300 MiB × warm + 0.3 GB × burst. Design 2 (supervisor-side compressed store) not needed on this evidence.
- 2026-10-01: no compressing block layer under swap (ZFS, dm-vdo), final. Fewer bytes per cold wake, if pursued, is a supervisor problem (which pages to prefetch), not a storage one. Kernel stays 6.8 on the reference host; a kernel change requires re-measuring the cold floor and available memory.
- 2026-10-01: no hardware escalation. The CX43 (8 shared vCPU, 16 GB, throttled NVMe) stays the reference platform: every number is proven on basic hardware, and the supervisor's job is to expose the knobs (resident share, burst target, warm floor, cold floor) so a provider tunes it to their tenants and their hosts. Faster disks and bigger boxes are the provider's multiplier, not our proof.
- 2026-10-01: KSM dropped. Resident share target 5 % (user decision: burst latency for waiting tenants outranks more resident cells). Cap lifted at wake is a requirement. Honest cold-burst-of-ten number on the CX43 volume: 8.5 / 16 / 19 s, bandwidth-bound.
- 2026-10-01: no KSM (5 % saving, needs capabilities); no lazy cold wake (22 s). Resident tier = paused cells capped at ~300 MiB, cap lifted at wake. Host sizing rule: RAM ≈ 1 GB OS + 50 MiB × cold cells + 300 MiB × resident cells + 0.75 GB × burst size.
- 2026-09-30 (night): memory tiers are now cold 150 MiB (swapfile), idle-alive/trimmed-warm ~300 MiB (soft limit, no compression), active = unlimited. zswap not used on this host class; may be enabled by providers with many active tenants.
- 2026-09-30 (later): compressed RAM tier closed for NVMe-class cloud hosts (best case slower than disk, competes with burst headroom). Warm tier gets a second level: trimmed warm (resident-paused cells reclaimed to a ~400 MiB floor at once, free because the pages are clean; 1.2 s wake) above cold (150 MiB floor under pressure).
- 2026-09-30: no compressed front tier (zswap/zram) on hosts with a swapfile: after a cell's first reclaim ~90 % of its pages stay on disk for good, so a front tier only holds the dirty set. Warm tier = resident under headroom; cold tier = swapfile. zswap optional, off by default.
- 2026-09-29 (evening): headroom target = simultaneous wakes × 0.75 GB (8 GB default on a 16 GB host). Measured at 100 cells: 3 GB made a cold burst of ten worse than no warm tier (25.6 s, memory stall); 8 GB gave 4–10.5 s. Warm tier is the remainder.
- 2026-09-29: thaw settle default 1 s (was 3 s), flag `-thaw-settle`; basis: 15/15 clean first turns at 1.5/1/0.5 s bounds with the tenant's default heartbeat.
- 2026-09-29: wake concurrency bounds page-in only (default 2) and the slot is held until the pages have landed (FIFO on a bandwidth-bound device). Reclaim policy is pressure-first: `-headroom-mib` keeps recently paused cells resident and reclaims longest-paused first when available memory drops below the target; `-reclaim-after` remains as the timed upper bound (recommend 30m with headroom on).
- 2026-09-28: no swap on ZFS. Two hard hangs on fleet-exp-1 (kernel 6.8, OpenZFS 2.2.2), one under ordinary supervisor reclaim. Compressed cold store remains a backlog item behind a mechanism that does not allocate on swap-out (dm-vdo on kernel ≥ 6.9, or supervisor-side user-space compression).
- 2026-09-28: hibernation store is a swapfile on the host's NVMe, not zram. Measured on fleet-exp-1: disk wake 1.5–2.0 s vs zram 2.3–2.5 s, and disk frees the 8 GB zram was holding, which is what removed the burst contention (10 wakes at 50 cells: 9/17/24 s vs 25/32/52 s). zram writeback as a cold tier rejected: page-at-a-time reads, 6.6–8.4 s wakes. Two-tier (zram warm + disk cold) deferred: only worth it if combined bandwidth is needed for bursts.
- 2026-09-26: no content-reading polling keeper; webhook-first with ingress secret verification, count-only keeper as opt-in only.
- 2026-09-19: OpenClaw fleet supervisor chosen over Paperclip (docs/opportunity-scout-2026-09-19.md).
- 2026-09-19: Hetzner CX43 (~EUR 16/mo) for the experiment host; AMD CPX plans avoided after June 2026 price rise.
- 2026-09-26: Hibernation is tiered (pause → CRIU → stop) after local measurement showed 70–90 s gateway start; readiness is HTTP /health.
- 2026-09-26: Phase 0 split into local correctness (0a) and Hetzner numbers (0b); upstream engagement (0c) scheduled after numbers exist.
